PCEStudio editor — paint tiles, choose palettes from the 512 colours, paint the map

Palettes — click a swatch, then a colour from the 512

VCE colour table (512 × 9-bit)

A swatch is one of a palette's 16 colours. Colour 0 of every palette (dashed) is the shared backdrop: the VDC shows CRAM $000 for every colour-0 pixel whatever palette the cell names. The BAT's palette field is 4 bits, so there are 16 palettes and a 17th is refused; a palette has 16 colours and a 17th is refused.

Tile

Click or drag to paint with the selected colour number. A tile is 8×8 pixels, each a 4-bit colour number 0–15; the 17th colour is refused.

Background map (BAT, 32×32 tiles)


The line at row 224 is the bottom of the 256×224 view; rows below it exist in the BAT and are not displayed at BYR = 0.

Sprite palettes — 16 × 16, CRAM $100–$1FF

Click a swatch, then a colour from the 512 above. A sprite pixel of colour 0 is transparent; entry 0 is the transparent entry and is never shown on a sprite (CRAM $100 is the background-off fill). A 17th colour in a sprite palette is refused.

Sprite pattern

Paint with the selected sprite-palette colour number (entry 0 is transparent). The sizes are the VDC's CGX/CGY: 16 or 32 wide, 16/32/64 tall; any other size is refused. Patterns are packed into VRAM cells after the tiles; one that would reach the SAT at $7F00 is refused.

Sprites — up to 64 in the SAT

X Y pattern palette

The VDC collects 16 strips of 16 px (256 px) a line in SAT order; sprites past that are dropped, and the budget shows the keeps and drops exactly as the renderer draws them. Lower SAT index wins an overlap; priority sets SPBG (shown over an opaque background).
